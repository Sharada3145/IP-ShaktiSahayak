# Trade Marks Act, 1999

## Overview

The Trade Marks Act, 1999 governs the registration and protection of trademarks in India. For Ayurvedic businesses, trademark registration is often the most accessible and important form of IP protection.

## Section 2 — Definitions

Section 2(1)(zb) defines "trade mark" as a mark capable of being represented graphically and which is capable of distinguishing the goods or services of one person from those of others. A mark may include a device, brand, heading, label, ticket, name, signature, word, letter, numeral, shape of goods, packaging, or combination of colours.

## Section 9 — Absolute Grounds for Refusal

Section 9(1) bars registration of trademarks that:
- Are devoid of distinctive character
- Consist exclusively of marks that designate the kind, quality, quantity, intended purpose, values, geographical origin, or time of production
- Consist exclusively of marks that have become customary in current language

For Ayurvedic products, generic Ayurvedic terms (e.g., "Chyawanprash," "Triphala," "Ashwagandha") cannot be registered as trademarks because they are descriptive or customary terms. However, distinctive brand names combined with these terms CAN be registered (e.g., "Dabur Chyawanprash").

## Section 11 — Relative Grounds for Refusal

Section 11 bars registration of a trademark that is identical or deceptively similar to an earlier trademark for the same or similar goods.

## Section 18 — Application for Registration

Section 18 provides for the application to register a trademark. Application can be made to the Registrar of Trade Marks (IP India).

## Section 28 — Rights Conferred by Registration

Section 28 confers on the registered proprietor the exclusive right to use the trademark in relation to the goods/services for which it is registered, and to obtain relief in case of infringement.

## Section 29 — Infringement

Section 29 defines infringement of a registered trademark and provides for civil remedies including injunction and damages.

## Trademark Strategy for Ayurvedic Businesses

1. **Brand Name**: Register a distinctive brand name as a trademark
2. **Logo/Device**: Register a unique logo or graphical element
3. **Product Line**: Register separate marks for different product lines
4. **Tagline**: Register distinctive taglines or slogans
5. **Packaging**: Consider 3D trademark for distinctive packaging shapes
6. **International Filing**: Use the Madrid Protocol for international trademark registration

### Do's:
- Choose distinctive, coined words (e.g., "Himalaya," "Patanjali")
- Combine descriptive terms with distinctive elements
- File early to establish priority
- Use the ™ symbol before registration and ® after

### Don'ts:
- Don't try to register generic Ayurvedic terms alone
- Don't use marks that are deceptively similar to existing brands
- Don't use geographical names unless through GI registration
- Don't use marks that are misleading about the nature of the product

## Section 47 — Removal from Register for Non-Use

Section 47 allows any aggrieved person to apply for removal of a trademark from the register if it has not been used for a continuous period of 5 years and 3 months. This underscores the importance of actual use of the registered trademark.
