# Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999

## Overview

The Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999 (GI Act) provides for the registration and protection of geographical indications in India. A Geographical Indication (GI) is a sign used on products that have a specific geographical origin and possess qualities or a reputation due to that place of origin.

## Section 2 — Definitions

Section 2(1)(e) defines "geographical indication" as an indication which identifies goods as agricultural goods, natural goods, or manufactured goods as originating, or manufactured in the territory of a country, or a region or locality in that territory, where a given quality, reputation, or other characteristic of such goods is essentially attributable to its geographical origin.

## Relevance to Ayurveda

GI registration is highly relevant for Ayurvedic products where the quality or reputation is linked to a specific geographical region. Examples include:

1. **Region-specific preparations**: Ayurvedic medicines prepared in specific regions using local ingredients and traditional methods
2. **Medicinal plants**: Plants with therapeutic properties linked to specific growing regions (e.g., Darjeeling Tea, Erode Turmeric)
3. **Traditional processing methods**: Products whose quality depends on traditional regional methods of preparation

### Registered Ayurveda-related GIs include:
- Erode Turmeric
- Lakadong Turmeric
- Wayanad Turmeric
- Alleppey Green Cardamom
- Coorg Green Cardamom
- Malabar Pepper
- Sikkim Large Cardamom
- Assam Orthodoxy Tea
- Various regional spices and medicinal plants

## Section 7-8 — Application for Registration

Section 7 provides that any association of persons, producers, organization, or authority established by or under any law can apply for registration of a GI. Section 8 sets out the application procedure.

For Ayurvedic products, an association of manufacturers or a producer group from the specific region would typically apply.

## Section 11 — Registration of GI

Section 11 provides for the registration of a GI after examination and publication. A registered GI is valid for 10 years and is renewable.

## Section 21-22 — Rights Conferred by Registration

Section 21 confers on the registered proprietor and authorized users the exclusive right to use the GI in relation to the goods. Section 22 prohibits the use of a registered GI by any person who is not an authorized user.

## IP Strategy for Ayurvedic GIs

GI registration can complement other IP protections for Ayurvedic products:

1. **Quality assurance**: GI registration establishes standards that all producers in the region must meet
2. **Market differentiation**: GI provides a legally protected marketing tool
3. **Complementary to trademarks**: A company can use both a GI and a trademark (e.g., "Erode Turmeric" GI + company brand name)
4. **No individual ownership**: GI is a collective right — it protects all qualified producers in the region
5. **Does not expire with non-use**: Unlike trademarks, GI cannot be cancelled for non-use
6. **International protection**: India is a member of the Lisbon Agreement for international GI protection
