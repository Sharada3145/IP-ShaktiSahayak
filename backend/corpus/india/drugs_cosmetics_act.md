# Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 — AYUSH Drug Provisions

The Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 (D&C Act) and the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945 (D&C Rules) govern the manufacture, sale, and distribution of drugs in India, including Ayurvedic, Siddha, and Unani (ASU) medicines.

## Classification of AYUSH Drugs

The D&C Act classifies Ayurvedic drugs into several categories, each with distinct regulatory requirements and IP implications:

### 1. Classical/Generic Medicines (Rule 158B)

Rule 158B covers Ayurvedic medicines that are described in the authoritative books specified in the First Schedule of the D&C Act. These include formulations from:
- Charaka Samhita
- Sushruta Samhita
- Ashtanga Hridaya / Ashtanga Sangraha
- Sharangdhara Samhita
- Bhavaprakasha
- Bhaishajya Ratnavali
- Yoga Ratnakara
- Other texts listed in the First Schedule

**Regulatory Requirements:**
- Manufacturing license from State Licensing Authority
- GMP compliance under Schedule T
- Must conform to standards in the Ayurvedic Pharmacopoeia of India (API)
- No clinical trial data required — traditional use evidence is sufficient
- Standard quality control tests as per pharmacopoeial monographs

**IP Implications:**
- Section 3(p) of Patents Act bars patenting as traditional knowledge
- TKDL provides defensive protection against misappropriation
- Trademark registration possible for brand name
- GI registration possible for region-specific preparations

### 2. Patent/Proprietary Medicines (Rule 158C)

Rule 158C covers Ayurvedic proprietary medicines — formulations NOT described in authoritative texts but bearing a brand name by the manufacturer.

**Regulatory Requirements:**
- Manufacturing license from State Licensing Authority
- Must declare all ingredients and their quantities
- 150 years of traditional use evidence OR limited safety data
- GMP compliance under Schedule T
- Labeling requirements including list of ingredients

**IP Implications:**
- Trademark is the primary IP protection
- Trade secret protection for proprietary formulation
- Patent possible if the formulation demonstrates genuine novelty and inventive step
- Design registration for unique packaging

### 3. New Drugs (Rule 170)

Rule 170 covers new AYUSH drugs — formulations not described in authoritative texts and which have NOT been marketed in India for a sufficient period.

**Regulatory Requirements:**
- Requires clinical trials (Phase I, II, III)
- Clinical Trial Registry-India (CTRI) registration mandatory
- CDSCO approval required
- Complete safety and efficacy data
- Post-marketing surveillance

**IP Implications:**
- HIGH patent potential for novel composition, process, or use
- Clinical trial data is a valuable IP asset
- ABS compliance under Biological Diversity Act is critical
- PCT filing for international patent protection

### 4. Phytopharmaceuticals

Phytopharmaceuticals are a category introduced to bridge traditional medicine and modern drug development. They are purified and standardized extracts from medicinal plants.

**Regulatory Requirements:**
- New drug pathway under D&C Act
- Safety and efficacy data required
- Standardization and quality control mandatory
- GMP compliance
- CDSCO approval required

**IP Implications:**
- Patent possible for extraction process and standardized composition
- Budapest Treaty relevant for microorganism/cell line deposits
- ABS compliance mandatory
- Plant variety protection may apply for novel cultivars

## Section 33EE — Central Government Powers for AYUSH

Section 33EE empowers the Central Government to regulate the manufacture, sale, and distribution of AYUSH drugs, including setting standards and prohibiting the manufacture/sale of drugs that do not meet prescribed standards.

## Schedule T — Good Manufacturing Practices for AYUSH

Schedule T prescribes the Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) standards for Ayurvedic, Siddha, and Unani drugs. Compliance is mandatory for all AYUSH drug manufacturers.

## Labeling Requirements

AYUSH drugs must comply with specific labeling requirements:
- Name of the product
- List of ingredients with quantities
- Date of manufacture and expiry
- Batch number
- Manufacturing license number
- Name and address of manufacturer
- Directions for use
- Therapeutic indications
- Warnings and contraindications (where applicable)
