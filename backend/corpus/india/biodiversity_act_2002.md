# Biological Diversity Act, 2002 (as amended 2023)

The Biological Diversity Act, 2002 (BD Act) is India's legislation implementing the Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD). It was significantly amended in 2023 (Biological Diversity (Amendment) Act, 2023) to simplify compliance, promote Ayurveda and traditional medicine, and facilitate access-and-benefit-sharing (ABS).

## Section 3 — Restriction on Access to Biological Resources by Foreign Persons

Section 3(1) provides that no person who is not a citizen of India, or a non-resident, or a body corporate not established in India, shall access any biological resource occurring in India for research, commercial utilization, bio-survey, or bio-utilization WITHOUT the prior approval of the National Biodiversity Authority (NBA).

Section 3(2) extends this restriction to collaborative research involving foreign institutions.

This is critical for international pharmaceutical and AYUSH companies seeking to commercialize Ayurvedic formulations using Indian biological resources.

## Section 4 — Results of Research Not Transferred Without NBA Approval

Section 4 provides that no person shall transfer the results of any research relating to biological resources occurring in India to any foreign person or entity without the prior approval of the NBA.

## Section 6 — Application for Intellectual Property Rights

Section 6(1) provides that no person shall apply for any intellectual property right, by whatever name called, in or outside India for any invention based on any research or information on a biological resource obtained from India without the prior approval of the NBA.

Under the 2023 Amendment, this requirement has been modified for codified traditional knowledge. Section 6 now exempts AYUSH practitioners and practitioners of traditional medicine who are Indian citizens and use codified traditional knowledge in certain ways.

## Section 7 — Prior Intimation to State Biodiversity Board

Section 7 requires that Indian citizens or body corporates registered in India give prior intimation to the State Biodiversity Board (SBB) before obtaining any biological resource for commercial utilization, bio-survey, or bio-utilization.

Under the 2023 Amendment, registered AYUSH practitioners and persons using codified traditional knowledge are required to give prior intimation rather than seek prior approval, simplifying the compliance process.

## Sections 19-21 — Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS)

### Section 19 — Approval by NBA

Section 19 requires the NBA to determine the terms of access, including the quantum of benefit-sharing, when granting approval under Sections 3, 4, or 6.

### Section 20 — Determination of Benefit Sharing

Section 20 provides that the NBA shall determine benefit-sharing in consultation with local bodies, including:
- Grant of joint ownership of intellectual property rights
- Transfer of technology
- Location of production, research, and development units in areas providing biological resources
- Association of Indian scientists, benefit claimers, and local people in research and development
- Monetary compensation through access fees, royalties, or milestone payments
- Payment to the National Biodiversity Fund

### Section 21 — Certain Provisions for Benefit Sharing

Section 21 provides that the NBA may direct a person paying royalties to also pay an amount to the NBA for benefit-sharing, to be deposited in the National Biodiversity Fund.

## 2023 Amendment — Key Changes for Ayurveda/AYUSH

The Biological Diversity (Amendment) Act, 2023 introduced several changes specifically relevant to Ayurveda:

1. **Codified Traditional Knowledge**: The amendment distinguishes between "codified traditional knowledge" (documented in texts like Charaka Samhita) and undocumented TK, with simplified compliance for the former.

2. **AYUSH Practitioners**: Registered AYUSH practitioners using codified traditional knowledge are given simplified access — prior intimation rather than prior approval.

3. **Facilitated Access**: The amendment encourages cultivation and sustainable use of medicinal plants, reducing barriers for legitimate Ayurvedic manufacturing.

4. **Benefit-Sharing by Indian Entities**: Indian companies using biological resources must share benefits with local communities through the State Biodiversity Boards.

5. **Decriminalization**: The amendment replaces criminal penalties with civil penalties (fines) for certain violations, making enforcement more proportionate.

## Biological Diversity Rules, 2024

The Biological Diversity Rules, 2024 provide the procedural framework for implementing the amended Act:

- Procedures for seeking NBA approval or giving prior intimation
- Forms for ABS applications
- Methodology for determining benefit-sharing
- Establishment and functioning of Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs)
- Guidelines for access to biological resources for research vs. commercial utilization
- Digital application and processing procedures
