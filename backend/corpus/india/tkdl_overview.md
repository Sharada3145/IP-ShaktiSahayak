# Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL)

## Overview

The Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL) is a pioneering Indian initiative that digitizes and classifies India's traditional medical knowledge in a format accessible to international patent examiners. It serves as a crucial defensive IP tool against the misappropriation of traditional knowledge through the patent system.

TKDL was established in 2001 as a collaboration between the Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR) and the Ministry of AYUSH. It systematically documents traditional knowledge from classical Ayurvedic, Unani, Siddha, and Yoga texts in multiple languages (English, French, German, Spanish, and Japanese) to serve as searchable prior art at patent offices worldwide.

## Purpose and Functionality

### Defensive Protection

The primary purpose of TKDL is to prevent the grant of wrong patents on traditional knowledge at international patent offices. Before TKDL, traditional knowledge documented in Sanskrit, Arabic, Tamil, or other Indian languages was not accessible to patent examiners, who typically search prior art in English and other European languages.

TKDL achieves this by:
1. Translating traditional formulations into patent-compatible formats
2. Using the Traditional Knowledge Resource Classification (TKRC), a structured classification system compatible with the International Patent Classification (IPC)
3. Making this information available to patent offices under access agreements

### TKDL has Successfully Challenged Patents Including:
- Turmeric wound healing patent at USPTO (revoked)
- Neem antifungal patent at EPO (revoked)
- Multiple patents based on traditional formulations at JPO, EPO, USPTO

## Traditional Knowledge Resource Classification (TKRC)

TKRC is a novel classification system developed for TKDL, based on the International Patent Classification (IPC) system. It allows patent examiners to search traditional knowledge using the same IPC codes they use for modern inventions.

TKRC structure example:
- A61K 36/00 — Medicinal preparations of undetermined constitution containing material from algae, lichens, fungi or plants
- K07 — Ayurvedic preparations (TKDL-specific codes)

## Coverage

TKDL currently documents:
- Approximately 4.5 lakh (450,000) formulations from Ayurvedic, Unani, Siddha, and Yoga texts
- Over 2,500 classical Ayurvedic texts analyzed
- Formulations classified by therapeutic area, ingredients, method of preparation, and dosage
- Cross-referenced with modern pharmacological data where available

## Access Agreements

TKDL is NOT publicly accessible. It is available to patent offices under Access Agreements that restrict its use to patent examination only. Access agreements have been signed with:
- European Patent Office (EPO)
- United States Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO)
- Japan Patent Office (JPO)
- Canadian Intellectual Property Office (CIPO)
- German Patent Office (DPMA)
- UK Intellectual Property Office (UKIPO)
- Australian IP Office (IP Australia)
- Other patent offices

## TKDL and Section 3(p) of Patents Act

TKDL records serve as prior art that directly supports the Section 3(p) bar on patenting traditional knowledge. When a patent application at any patent office relates to a traditional formulation, TKDL records can be cited as prior art to:
- Reject the application during examination
- Support opposition proceedings under Section 25
- Support revocation proceedings under Section 64

## IP Strategy Implications for Ayurvedic Innovations

For Ayurvedic innovators, TKDL has important strategic implications:

1. **Classical formulations are documented prior art**: If your formulation is identical or substantially similar to a TKDL record, it is unlikely to be patentable in any jurisdiction.

2. **Modifications may still be patentable**: If you make a genuinely inventive modification to a classical formulation (novel extraction, new combination, new delivery system), it may be patentable — but the innovation must go beyond mere "aggregation" of known properties.

3. **Freedom to operate**: The existence of TKDL records may help establish freedom to operate for manufacturing classical formulations, as it demonstrates they are public domain knowledge.

4. **International protection**: TKDL's access agreements with international patent offices help prevent biopiracy and misappropriation of Indian traditional knowledge globally.
