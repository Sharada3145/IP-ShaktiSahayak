# WIPO Treaty on Intellectual Property, Genetic Resources and Associated Traditional Knowledge (GRATK Treaty, 2024)

## Overview

Adopted in May 2024, the WIPO GRATK Treaty is a landmark international agreement that bridges the intellectual property system (patents) with the biodiversity system (CBD/Nagoya Protocol). It addresses the problem of "biopiracy" — the misappropriation of genetic resources (GRs) and traditional knowledge (TK) through the patent system.

For Ayurveda, this treaty is highly significant because it creates a mandatory international requirement for patent applicants to disclose the origin of Indian traditional knowledge and genetic resources when applying for patents in other countries.

## Core Requirement: Mandatory Disclosure (Article 3)

Article 3 establishes the core obligation of the Treaty: a mandatory disclosure requirement (MDR) in patent applications.

1. **Genetic Resources (GRs)**: Where a claimed invention in a patent application is *materially/directly based on* genetic resources, each Contracting Party shall require applicants to disclose:
   - The country of origin of the genetic resources; or
   - If the country of origin is not known, the source of the genetic resources.

2. **Associated Traditional Knowledge (TK)**: Where a claimed invention in a patent application is *materially/directly based on* traditional knowledge associated with genetic resources, each Contracting Party shall require applicants to disclose:
   - The Indigenous Peoples or local community that provided the traditional knowledge; or
   - If that is not known, the source of the traditional knowledge.

3. **Declaration of Unknown Origin/Source**: If none of the above information is known to the applicant, they must make a declaration to that effect.

### What "Materially/Directly Based On" Means
The treaty specifies that the GR or TK must have been necessary or material to the development of the claimed invention, and the claimed invention must depend on the specific properties of the GR or TK.

## Exceptions and Limitations (Article 4)

Article 4 allows Contracting Parties to provide exceptions to the disclosure requirement in certain special cases, provided they do not unreasonably prejudice the protection of GRs and associated TK.

## Sanctions and Remedies (Article 5)

Article 5 deals with the consequences of failing to comply with the disclosure requirement:

1. **Pre-Grant**: Contracting Parties must provide an opportunity for applicants to rectify a failure to include the required disclosure before refusing a patent application.
2. **Post-Grant**: Crucially, Contracting Parties shall NOT revoke or invalidate a patent solely on the basis of a failure to disclose the required information, unless there has been fraudulent intent.
3. **Alternative Sanctions**: Parties must provide adequate, effective, and proportionate legal, administrative, or policy measures (which could include fines or other penalties) to address failures to disclose.

## Information Systems (Article 6)

Article 6 encourages the establishment of information systems (such as databases) of GRs and TK, in consultation with Indigenous Peoples and local communities. The Indian Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL) is a prime example of such an information system, and the treaty encourages patent offices to use such databases for prior art searches.

## Strategic Implications for Ayurveda

1. **Global Protection**: Once implemented by member states, Indian Ayurvedic knowledge and resources will have an additional layer of protection globally. Foreign inventors using Indian medicinal plants or classical formulations will be legally required to disclose India as the source.
2. **ABS Linkage**: While the treaty itself does not mandate Access and Benefit-Sharing (ABS), the disclosure requirement acts as a powerful "checkpoint." It makes it visible to provider countries (like India) when their resources/TK are being patented, enabling them to enforce their national ABS laws (like the Biological Diversity Act).
3. **Defensive vs. Positive Protection**: The TKDL provides *defensive* protection (preventing patents via prior art). The GRATK Treaty provides a form of *positive* transparency, forcing applicants to be upfront about the resources they used.
