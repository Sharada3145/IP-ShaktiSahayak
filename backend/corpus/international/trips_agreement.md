# Agreement on Trade-Related Aspects of Intellectual Property Rights (TRIPS)

## Overview

The TRIPS Agreement is an international legal agreement between all the member nations of the World Trade Organization (WTO). It sets down minimum standards for many forms of intellectual property (IP) regulation as applied to nationals of other WTO Members. For Ayurveda and traditional medicine, TRIPS establishes the baseline for what must be patentable globally, but also provides flexibilities that countries like India use to protect traditional knowledge.

## Article 27 — Patentable Subject Matter

Article 27.1 requires that patents shall be available for any inventions, whether products or processes, in all fields of technology, provided that they are new, involve an inventive step, and are capable of industrial application.

### Exclusions from Patentability (TRIPS Flexibilities)

Article 27.2 allows Members to exclude from patentability inventions, the prevention within their territory of the commercial exploitation of which is necessary to protect ordre public or morality, including to protect human, animal or plant life or health or to avoid serious prejudice to the environment.

Article 27.3(a) allows Members to exclude diagnostic, therapeutic and surgical methods for the treatment of humans or animals. (India uses this flexibility in Section 3(i) of the Patents Act, meaning Ayurvedic treatment methods cannot be patented).

Article 27.3(b) allows Members to exclude plants and animals other than micro-organisms, and essentially biological processes for the production of plants or animals other than non-biological and microbiological processes. However, Members MUST provide for the protection of plant varieties either by patents or by an effective *sui generis* system or by any combination thereof. (India implemented the Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Act, 2001 to fulfill this requirement).

## Article 29 — Conditions on Patent Applicants

Article 29 requires that an applicant for a patent shall disclose the invention in a manner sufficiently clear and complete for the invention to be carried out by a person skilled in the art.

This requirement is central to the debate on disclosing the origin of genetic resources and traditional knowledge. Many developing nations argue that "complete disclosure" should include disclosing where the biological resources or traditional knowledge were sourced from.

## Article 39 — Protection of Undisclosed Information (Trade Secrets)

Article 39 requires Members to protect undisclosed information (trade secrets) against being disclosed to, acquired by, or used by others without their consent in a manner contrary to honest commercial practices.

For Ayurvedic proprietary medicines where the formulation is not patented, trade secret protection under the principles of Article 39 is often the most critical form of IP protection alongside trademarks.

## TRIPS and Traditional Knowledge (TK)

The TRIPS Agreement itself does not explicitly mention "traditional knowledge" or "access and benefit-sharing" (ABS). This has led to tension between the TRIPS framework (which promotes private IP rights) and the Convention on Biological Diversity (which asserts sovereign rights over biological resources and requires ABS).

Recent international efforts, such as the WIPO GRATK Treaty (2024), aim to bridge this gap by introducing mandatory disclosure requirements in the patent system for inventions based on genetic resources and associated TK.
